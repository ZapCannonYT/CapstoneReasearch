# Muscle-Tendon Force Estimation and Neuromuscular Solver Models

## 1. Executive Summary & The Muscle Redundancy Indeterminacy
To assess internal muscle strain, fatigue, and joint contact loads during exercise, the 3D Musculoskeletal Digital Twin (MS-DT) must estimate the force generated by individual skeletal muscles. However, the human musculoskeletal system exhibits profound mechanical actuator redundancy: across the lower extremity, over 40 distinct muscle-tendon units (MTUs) actuate only 7 primary degrees of freedom. Consequently, infinitely many combinations of muscle recruitment can satisfy the equations of motion for any observed exercise movement.

This document formalizes the mathematical frameworks used by the digital twin to resolve muscle indeterminacy, calibrate Hill-type muscle models, and compute real-time individual muscle forces.

## 2. The Hill-Type Muscle-Tendon Model

```
                              Muscle Fiber Complex
         +-------------------------------------------------------------+
         |                                                             |
         |        +------ Active Contractile Element (CE) -----+       |
         |        |   F_CE = a(t) * F0_M * f_L(l_M) * f_V(v_M) |       |
         |        +--------------------------------------------+       |
  -------+                                                             +------- Tendon (SEE) -------
         |        +------ Passive Elastic Element (PEE) -------+       |    F_SEE = F0_M * f_T(l_T)
         |        |   F_PE = F0_M * f_PE(l_M)                  |       |
         |        +--------------------------------------------+       |
         |                                                             |
         +-------------------- Pennation Angle (alpha) ---------------+
```

The tensile force $F_m^{MT}$ exerted by muscle-tendon actuator $m$ is modeled via the 3-element Hill model:

$$F_m^{MT}(t) = \left[ F_{\text{CE}, m}(a_m, \tilde{l}_{M, m}, \tilde{v}_{M, m}) + F_{\text{PE}, m}(\tilde{l}_{M, m}) \right] \cdot \cos \alpha_m = F_{\text{SEE}, m}(\tilde{l}_{T, m})$$

Where:
- $a_m(t) \in [0, 1]$ is the dimensionless **muscle activation state** derived from activation dynamics or optimization.
- $F_{o, m}^M$ is the **maximum isometric muscle force** (derived from anatomical physiological cross-sectional area - PCSA).
- $\tilde{l}_{M, m} = \frac{l_{M, m}}{l_{o, m}^M}$ is the normalized muscle fiber length relative to optimal fiber length.
- $\tilde{v}_{M, m} = \frac{v_{M, m}}{v_{\max, m}^M}$ is the normalized fiber contraction velocity.
- $f_L(\tilde{l}_M)$ and $f_V(\tilde{v}_M)$ are non-linear empirical force-length and force-velocity relationships:
  - Concentric shortening reduces force output ($f_V < 1.0$).
  - Eccentric lengthening yields force potentiation ($f_V \le 1.4 - 1.8$).
- $F_{\text{SEE}, m}$ is the non-linear tendon elastic strain force, governed by tendon slack length $l_{s, m}^T$:
  $$f_T(\tilde{l}_T) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{for } \tilde{l}_T \le 1.0 \\ c_1 \cdot (\tilde{l}_T - 1)^2 & \text{for toe region} \\ k_{\text{linear}} \cdot (\tilde{l}_T - \tilde{l}_{T, \text{toe}}) & \text{for linear elastic region} \end{cases}$$

## 3. Resolving Muscle Redundancy: Static Optimization vs. EMG-Informed Solvers

```
[ Net Joint Torques tau(t) ]                [ Muscle Moment Arms r_m(q) ]
              \                                          /
               \                                        /
                v                                      v
      [ Constrained Optimization Engine / Quadratic Programming ]
                      ^                        ^
                      |                        |
         [ Minimizing Sum a_m^p ]    [ Optional: Bounded by sEMG u_norm(t) ]
                                 |
                                 v
          [ Individual Muscle Forces F_m^MT(t) (Vastus, Gluteus, Hamstrings) ]
```

### 3.1 Non-Linear Static Optimization (SO)
Static optimization resolves muscle forces at each instantaneous time frame by minimizing physiological fatigue while satisfying net joint moments:

$$\min_{\mathbf{a}} J = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \left( a_m \right)^p \quad (p = 2 \text{ or } 3)$$

**Subject to:**
1. **Dynamic Moment Equilibrium:**
   $$\sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathbf{r}_{m, j}(\mathbf{q}) \cdot F_m^{MT}(a_m, l_M, v_M) = \tau_j(t) \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$$
2. **Biological Activation Limits:**
   $$0 \le a_m \le 1 \quad \forall m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$$

*Computational Advantage:* Solved in $<15\,\text{ms}$ via sequential quadratic programming (SQP), enabling real-time deployment.

### 3.2 Calibrated EMG-Informed Neuromusculoskeletal Modeling (CEINMS)
While Static Optimization assumes pure metabolic efficiency, human motor control frequently employs **antagonistic co-contraction** to stabilize joints under heavy or uncertain loads (e.g., co-activating hamstrings during heavy knee extension squats):
- In the EMG-informed pipeline, normalized sEMG envelope signals $u_{\text{norm}, m}(t)$ directly drive the activation dynamics of measured superficial muscles (Vastus Lateralis, Biceps Femoris, Gastrocnemius).
- Deep or unmeasured muscles are estimated through hybrid tracking optimization that balances torque equilibrium and measured EMG tracking:
  $$\min_{\mathbf{a}} J_{\text{hybrid}} = \sum_{j=1}^n \left( \tau_j - \tau_{j, \text{est}} \right)^2 + \beta \sum_{m \in \text{measured}} \left( a_m - a_{\text{sEMG}, m} \right)^2 + \gamma \sum_{m=1}^M a_m^2$$

## 5. Algorithmic Solve Times & Real-Time Performance
- **Static Optimization (SO):** Solved via interior-point solvers in OpenSim / SimTK: **$12 - 22\,\text{ms}$** per frame.
- **Neural Network Surrogate Solvers:** Deep multilayer perceptrons (MLP) trained on precomputed OpenSim SO databases infer all 40+ muscle forces from kinematics in **$< 1.5\,\text{ms}$**, unlocking instantaneous edge simulation.
